RESEARCH NOTE 001 / SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICE MODELLING

What happens when we strain an atomically thin transistor?

A numerical study comparing strain-dependent characteristics of monolayer and bilayer molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂) field-effect transistors.

JUNAID ZIAFINAL YEAR PROJECT · UNIVERSITI MALAYA2026

01 — THE QUESTION

Can mechanical strain meaningfully alter electrical performance?

Atomically thin semiconductors are attractive research platforms because their electronic properties can respond to external influences. I investigated one such influence — tensile strain — and compared the response of single-layer and double-layer MoS₂ transistor channels.

The goal was not to report a fabricated device. It was to use a physics-based simulation to explore how the assumptions and semiconductor material parameters translated into measurable device-level trends.

“Compare the same physical question across two channel thicknesses — and account for the differences.”
02 — THE NUMERICAL MODEL

Starting with the physics.

Using COMSOL Multiphysics and the Semiconductor Module, I developed two-dimensional device models based on electrostatic potential and charge-carrier continuity relationships. I examined the current response while varying tensile strain from 0 to 0.7%.

03 — THE FINDINGS

Both channels improved, but not by the same amount.

At the simulated endpoint of 0.7% tensile strain, the on-state current increased in both channel types. The monolayer showed the greater relative gain, while the bilayer maintained the greater absolute current.

04 — INTERPRETATION

A useful answer should explain why.

My emphasis was on translating material-property assumptions into device-level electrical behaviour, then comparing the strength of that response between layer counts. The task was to understand the relationship, not only generate curves.

This project developed my confidence in physics-guided modelling, simulation debugging, evaluating parameter sensitivity, and communicating results with appropriate caveats. It also demonstrated why a single favourable metric should not be presented as a complete performance improvement.

05 — WHERE THIS LEADS

From undergraduate modelling to deeper device research.

I'm interested in continuing research on semiconductor device behaviour, electronic materials, and numerical techniques that connect physical models with engineering design. Future work could investigate additional transport assumptions, broader bias conditions, and comparisons with experimental literature and measurements.

This case study represents undergraduate research work and does not claim a published journal article. A research publication is under consideration with the academic supervisor.
